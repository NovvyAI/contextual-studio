# Mobile-game product analysis rubric

## Product layers

Analyze the game from the inside out:

1. **Atomic input:** tap, drag, swipe, hold, draw, merge, place, choose, aim, or idle.
2. **Move rule:** what can move, where it can move, and what blocks it.
3. **Success condition:** clear, match, sort, build, survive, reach, rescue, collect, or optimize.
4. **Pressure or failure:** timer, move limit, blocked board, resource loss, enemy, imperfect score, or no explicit failure.
5. **Immediate feedback:** animation, haptic, sound, combo, transformation, cleanup, destruction, or character response.
6. **Level loop:** action → solve → reward → next level.
7. **Meta loop:** renovation, collection, story, upgrade, customization, map, event, social competition, or live operations.
8. **Economy and monetization:** currency sources and sinks, boosters, lives or energy, rewarded ads, interstitials, purchases, subscription, and bundles.
9. **Narrative wrapper:** story used to motivate play but not itself the dominant interaction.

State missing information rather than filling gaps with genre conventions.

## Selling-point hierarchy

Evaluate each candidate selling point from 1–5 on:

- **Truth:** how directly the product supports it.
- **Demonstrability:** whether viewers understand it in 1–3 seconds.
- **Distinctiveness:** whether it differs from close competitors.
- **Emotional pull:** whether it promises relief, mastery, care, appetite, fantasy, status, curiosity, or justice.
- **Depth:** whether it can sustain play beyond the ad impression.

Prioritize promises scoring well across several dimensions. A vivid theme can be a strong hook even when mechanically undifferentiated; say so explicitly.

## Store creative decoding

For every asset, ask:

- What stops the scroll?
- What is the first verb implied to the player?
- Is the mechanic visible or only asserted by text?
- Is the image showing core gameplay, meta progression, story, or aspiration?
- What emotional state appears before and after the player's intervention?
- Which color, object, character, arrow, hand, or contrast directs attention?
- Would a new user expect this exact experience after installation?

Map the full gallery to a funnel:

```text
attention → comprehension → desire → depth → trust → install
```

## Review analysis

Sample by sentiment and recency. Group comments into themes such as:

- mechanic satisfaction;
- visual or audio pleasure;
- challenge balance;
- ad frequency;
- monetization friction;
- technical stability;
- content depth;
- misleading marketing;
- progression loss;
- accessibility;
- support quality.

Report review observations as signals, not measured prevalence, unless systematic data is available.

## Acquisition-creative taxonomy

Classify observable or proposed creatives as:

- pure mechanic demonstration;
- satisfying transformation or cleanup;
- fail-and-correct challenge;
- rescue or care dilemma;
- renovation before/after;
- narrative cliffhanger;
- UGC reaction or testimonial;
- appetite or sensory showcase;
- collection or customization reveal;
- competitive challenge;
- contextual crossover.

For each direction, identify hook, player action, payoff, product fidelity, and expectation risk.

## Evidence strength

Prefer evidence in this order for product facts:

1. current official store page;
2. official developer materials or in-game capture;
3. current official social or ad-library creative;
4. reputable gameplay recording;
5. aggregated reviews or analytics providers;
6. individual review, forum comment, or search snippet.

Lower-ranked evidence can reveal useful hypotheses but should not override direct product evidence without explaining the discrepancy.
